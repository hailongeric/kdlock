The baseline KD rwsem added sleeping admission around the accepted KD mutex, but its admission wait path was expensive for these short critical sections. This is a finding about that rwsem implementation and these workloads. It does not establish that KD rwlock is generally slow, or that sleeping is the only cost.

In baseline `485729f`, the reader count, writer-admission count and single `drain` waitqueue shared a cacheline. Reader entry/exit, writer reservations and waitqueue locking therefore updated neighboring state. Both admission waiters and writers waiting for readers used that same queue. The last reader and the final writer reservation called `wake_up_all()` on it, although those events enabled different predicates. After an initial failed condition, acquisition entered `wait_event()` without a separate optimistic-polling stage. Enqueueing and rechecking can avoid an actual sleep, but a woken task that loses admission must retry and can enqueue again. This creates both scheduling work and active synchronization work: shared-count updates, waitqueue locking, wake callbacks and renewed admission races. The baseline and selected implementations are retained by source identity; the maintained [rwsem provider](../../../../../kernel_module/kd_rwsem.c) and [object layout](../../../../../include/kd_rwsem.h) show the selected path.

These are diagnostic counts from [diagnostics.json](diagnostics.json), not exclusive CPU costs or lock latencies. Profiling changed execution cost and covered all CPUs/tasks during measurement, including client startup, counter reads and background activity; preparation, warmup and cleanup were excluded. Nested functions overlap, so their times or counts must not be summed as independent costs. Native uses its own rwsem paths rather than the provider's generic waitqueue admission: both profiles recorded approximately 0.799 `rwsem_down_read_slowpath` calls and 0.200 `rwsem_down_write_slowpath` calls per operation. Thus a low generic-waitqueue count does not mean Native never contended. Baseline KD profile sessions returned no JIT bodies, whereas all three selected KD sessions did. The reduction supports avoiding frequent waitqueue/scheduler work, but does not isolate each code change or delegation's contribution. Both profile captures restored tracing and remain outside throughput scoring.

The same profile also exposes active admission contention: selected KD has about 2.227 `kd_up_read` calls/op, compared with 0.804 before, although completed reads remain about 0.8/op. This function also rolls back failed reader admission. Its count therefore cannot be interpreted as successful reader unlocks alone; the selected path trades some sleeping/wakeup work for repeated active admission attempts.

TCLock is a different semantic reference here. Its [rwlock implementation](../../../../../tclock_backend/komb_rwlock.c) admits readers through a combined reader/writer count, uses a spinning gate for slow readers and writers, and executes queued writer critical sections through native stack switching in `execute_cs()`/`komb_context_switch()`. Slow readers can queue for admission, but release that gate before returning to execute their reader payload on their own task. The [benchmark wrappers](../../../../../workload_modules/rwlock_bench/direct.h) disable preemption across its read/write pairs. [KD rwlock](../../../../../include/kd_rwlock.h) also uses reader/writer count admission and a spinning gate, with KD JIT writer execution when admitted. These rwlock bodies cannot sleep. The [rwsem shell](../../../../../workload_modules/rwlock_bench/direct_rwsem.h), in contrast, calls sleeping Native or KD semaphore APIs; the [build configuration](../../../../../workload_modules/rwlock_bench/Makefile) explicitly rejects TCLock for rwsem because this port has no sleeping TCLock rwsem implementation. The separate Native/TCLock/KD rwlock measurements can compare those spinning implementations on these non-sleeping payloads. They are not interchangeable rwsem measurements or evidence of support for sleeping holders, cancellation and downgrade.

The implementation differences relevant to these measurements are:

| Mechanism | [TCLock spinning rwlock](../../../../../tclock_backend/komb_rwlock.c) | Baseline KD rwsem (`485729f`) | [Selected KD rwsem](../../../../../kernel_module/kd_rwsem.c) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reader admission | Increment combined count; test writer bits; slow readers withdraw and pass through the spinning gate. | Check open phase, increment reader count, then recheck phase; roll back on conflict. | Same exact admission checks; reader count and phase occupy separate aligned regions. |
| Waiting and queues | Spin on count/gate; no sleeping waitqueue in this path. | `wait_event()` after failed predicate; one queue for admission and reader drain, with wake-all on either event. | Up to 16,384 uninterruptible condition probes, with early `need_resched()` exit, then `wait_event()`; separate admission/drain queues and wake targets. |
| Writer gate and execution | Uncontended count CAS; contended gate serializes writers and can execute queued writer bodies by native stack switching. | Ordinary writer uses the KD mutex locally; outer writer reserves a phase and drains readers before KD mutex publication; admitted queued bodies can run through JIT. | Same writer protocol, execution and drain-before-publication ordering; ordinary reservation adds a read hint before its authoritative CAS. |
| Sleeping, cancellation and downgrade | Benchmark pairs disable preemption; no sleeping, interruptible/killable acquisition or downgrade API is supplied by this rwlock port. | Sleeping readers and physical owners are supported; ordinary APIs provide interruptible/killable acquisition and downgrade. Published outer producers remain pinned and cannot cancel or downgrade. | Same contract and cancellation rollback; polling is before publication, and interruptible/killable paths retain their sleeping waits. |

The selected candidate, archived as `768fc20cd981bfe28cc223825d25b5c9c2552757`, changes only the rwsem admission/wait layer and its queue-role probes. Reader counts, admission and both queues occupy separate 64-byte-aligned regions. `phase_wait` contains admission waiters; `reader_drain` contains writers that already own a reservation and need readers to finish. The final reader wakes only the latter, while phase release wakes only the former. Uninterruptible callers first try at most 16,384 condition probes, stop early for `need_resched()`, and otherwise retain the normal `wait_event()` fallback. Ordinary writer admission also reads the phase before attempting its authoritative compare/exchange. That hint affects the ordinary API, not the timed outer-writer shell. The budget is an experimentally chosen tuning for this captured platform, not a universal optimum or fixed time bound; the outer-reservation predicate retains its internal compare/exchange retries. Interruptible and killable paths keep their sleeping cancellation behavior. Readers acquire on their own tasks; the rejected pre-granted-reader experiment is not part of this candidate.

The separate state regions and additional waitqueue have a per-object memory cost. In the captured baseline and selected benchmark modules, `struct kd_rwsem` grows from **384 to 512 bytes**: **128 extra bytes, or one third**, with **128-byte alignment** unchanged. DWARF type sizes and the `rb_lock` ELF symbol sizes agree, and both module hashes match their frozen binary manifests; the [object-layout receipt](object-layout.json) records this offline inspection. Deployments with many semaphore objects must also assess the additional storage and cache-capacity cost. These contention benchmarks do not measure that deployment-wide tradeoff.

The [outer-writer wrapper](../../../../../include/kd_rwsem_direct.h) still reserves admission and drains every admitted reader before entering the unchanged KD mutex macro. A sleeping reader can therefore resume on the same CPU before a producer becomes pinned. An ordinary writer retains exclusive admission through its local gate ownership, and downgrade publishes its reader count before reopening admission. The phase reservation is released on the origin only after the matching mutex unlock returns. KD physical ownership, legacy queue/batching, ABI2, pinned published waiters, immutable images, instruction admission, source leases, unlock acknowledgements and frame retirement are unchanged. The [design guide](../../../../../docs/design/rwsem_implementation.md) describes the ordering and validation boundaries.

The focused c01 screen also shows that the benefit is not solely JIT execution. At 60 workers and 1% writes, the selected KD whole-run median was **7.780053 Mops/s**, compared with **1.068946 Mops/s** in the fresh baseline screen; all three KD repetitions in both versions had zero JIT entries and completions. At 20% writes, the selected KD whole-run median was **2.216555 Mops/s**, with exactly **102,453 / 125,105 / 114,770** matching JIT entries and completions across its three repetitions; the fresh baseline median was **0.400351 Mops/s** with zero JIT bodies. These are source-distinct focused screens, not pooled full-grid measurements. The write-heavy improvement can involve both the admission changes and naturally admitted delegation; those observations do not isolate each contribution. The existing heat and instruction-admission policies were retained. [screen-points.csv](screen-points.csv) and [sessions.csv](sessions.csv) retain the candidate histories, individual repetitions and execution groups.

The completed full grid confirms the high-count improvement independently of that screen. Across the six 60-worker cases, selected KD is **5.10–7.42× the initial KD** and **1.546–2.352× contemporary Native** on median whole throughput. All 1%-write cases remain `NO_JIT`; all 20%-write cases return delegated bodies. Over the full 126 points, 110 meet both observed median-whole and best-peak criteria versus initial KD, 12 fail and four cannot form homogeneous comparisons because execution statuses split. Against Native the counts are 117 passes, six failures and three splits. Every planned session completed; a split is not a skipped test.

The failures against the initial KD are all at one to five workers, and several are severe. At 20% writes, two-worker c01 changes from **35.137 to 10.065 Mops/s** and c08 from **32.149 to 8.859 Mops/s**, reductions of about **71.4% and 72.4%**. At three workers they fall from 18.822 to 7.982 and 18.794 to 8.035 Mops/s. Every repetition in these four old/new groups is `NO_JIT`, and every new warmed peak is below every old peak. Delegation cannot explain those differences. Median worker CPU cost rises from **40.6 to 198.6 ns/op** for two-worker c01 and **42.8 to 225.6 ns/op** for c08; each new worker consumes nearly two CPU-seconds per two-second session, versus roughly 1.2–1.5 before. These values sum worker CPU time only, not lock latency or system-wide CPU consumption.

Raw samples show every worker making progress in every eligible 10-ms interval in those two/three-worker cases. The baseline is somewhat less balanced, but a stalled worker does not explain its higher score at that resolution. The evidence is consistent with extra simultaneous busy contention from polling. Baseline blocking may allow cheaper bursts with less simultaneous contention; that is an inference, because no low-count scheduler trace isolated sleeping, other scheduling effects or sub-10-ms bursts. The 60-worker profiles cannot prove the low-count cause. The [targeted review](final-evidence-review.md) retains ranges, worker observations and all regressions. This tuning improves the high-contention cases while leaving a substantial low-count tradeoff; it is not uniformly faster.

The separate spinning reference also qualifies the original KD rwlock premise. On its 30 points, unchanged KD rwlock meets both observed throughput criteria at **29/30 against Native and 28/30 against TCLock**. At 60 workers it exceeds TCLock in five of six cases; RCUHT/1% is **14.326 versus 14.933 Mops/s**. The additional miss against both is c08/20% at one worker. These measurements use this source, topology and non-sleeping shell; they do not erase historical results from other configurations. TCLock's stack-delegation share remains unmeasured, and its runner `NATIVE` JIT label only means that KD witnesses do not apply.

There is also an unresolved repetition/order effect that must remain visible. Native c01/1%/60-worker rates in the fresh baseline screen were **0.777417 / 3.454906 / 3.563103 Mops/s**; the selected screen recorded **0.760028 / 3.613400 / 3.444056 Mops/s**. The first-sample reduction therefore already existed in the baseline, rather than first appearing with a KD change. Native executable text and relocations matched across the checked builds, and tracing was verified inactive. Identical governor policy and zero sampled thermal-throttle counts do not establish identical effective frequencies throughout each run. Load/frequency interaction is plausible but has not been isolated as the cause. All repetitions remain in the evidence and medians, and the later baseline-repeat screen is reported separately; neither unfavorable samples nor rejected candidates are discarded or normalized away.
